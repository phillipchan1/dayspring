A photo is hers. A camera icon is ours.

Day One and Diarly lead with the photo: the list becomes an album. Dayspring already has a rule for what goes on a page, written into PageCard.tsx:

Nothing goes on a page except her words, her date and her markings.

A photo she put on the page passes that rule. An icon we draw beside it doesn't. So the question isn't whether to show it. It's how much of the photo itself the list can carry from this far back without becoming an album. Switch directions below. On a computer, hover a row that has a photo.

The Dayspring way

  1. At rest, a print the size of a grain of rice, in the photo's own color. Her photo, seen from far back. Not our glyph. A set of three fans into three prints, the way D-033 already treats photos that touch.
  2. Step closer and it becomes the photo. Hover on a computer and the set rises as rows of equal height, every photo whole, with her caption under it if she wrote one.
  3. "Photos" joins Look for. Lighting dims the rest instead of hiding them, so the photographed days stand out against the years around them. The founder’s archive holds 30 photos in 3,146 pages (D-033), so a photographed day is a landmark: "the day I took a picture of the sunset" is how a half-remembered page gets found.

Sample pages and photos are invented for this prototype. Each photo is drawn on a canvas, and its print color is the average of those pixels, which is exactly the color the app's analyzeImage stores when a photo is added.
