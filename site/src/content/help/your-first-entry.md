Press **New entry** and start typing. That's genuinely all there is to it.

There's no title field to fill in first, no mood to pick, no tags to choose.
The first line becomes the title on its own, so just write the sentence you
came to write.

## It saves itself

You'll never look for a save button. Dayspring writes as you go, and a small
indicator tells you where things stand.

It also works offline. On a plane, on the underground, out of signal — keep
writing. It's stored on your device and syncs the moment you're back. You can
close the app mid-sentence and nothing is lost.

## Type `/` when you want more than words

On a new line, type `/` and a menu opens. It's how you put anything on the page
that isn't plain prose — a passage of scripture, a prayer, a photo, a heading, a
list.

There are {{counts.slash-commands}} of them. Type a few letters to filter:
`/scr` gets you scripture, `/pray` a prayer.

The full list is on [the slash menu page](/help/slash-menu), but you don't need
to learn it. Open the menu and read.

## Formatting

Select any text and a small bar appears with bold, italic, highlight and the
rest. Or use the shortcuts you already know — <kbd>⌘B</kbd>, <kbd>⌘I</kbd>.

Dayspring stores your writing as Markdown, but it hides the symbols. When you
bold a word, you see a bold word — not `**asterisks**`. The characters are
there in the file, just not in your face. [More on
formatting](/help/formatting-and-markdown).

## Writing about a different day

Wrote something on Tuesday about Sunday? Open the entry's menu and choose
**Change date**. The entry moves to where it belongs, and everything that reads
your journal back to you — the Ascent, the Lamp — reads it in the right place.

## What happens next

Nothing you have to do. As entries accumulate, the other surfaces begin to have
something to show: [the Lamp](/help/the-lamp) lights up the parts of scripture
you've been living in, [the Altar](/help/the-altar) gathers what you've prayed
about, and [the Ascent](/help/the-ascent) starts showing you the shape of a
week, then a month.

A handful of entries is enough for those to become interesting. You don't have
to write every day, and nothing scolds you if you don't.
