Dayspring is a journal you write in like any other. The difference is what it does
with what you've written.

Most journals are a drawer. You put pages in, and the pages sit there. Dayspring
keeps the drawer — your writing is yours, plainly stored, exportable any time —
but it also reads back. Over months it can show you which passages of scripture
you keep returning to, which prayers you've prayed more than once and what came
of them, and what a whole season looked like from above.

None of that is invented. Everything Dayspring shows you traces back to something
you actually wrote, quoted as you wrote it.

## The four places you'll spend time

**The page.** A quiet editor. Type `/` for scripture, a prayer, a photo, or one
of the contemplative practices. Nothing else asks for your attention.

**The Lamp.** Every book of the Bible, lit by how often you've written near it.
Over a year it becomes a map of where you've actually been living.

**The Altar.** The things you've prayed about, gathered by subject, with whatever
you later wrote about them alongside.

**The Ascent.** Your writing seen from four heights — a week, a month, a quarter,
a year. The higher you stand, the more the shape shows.

## What it will never do

It won't score your spiritual life. There's no streak, no badge, no gentle nudge
that you've missed a day. It won't preach at you, and it won't tell you what your
writing means.

If it can't point at a sentence you wrote, it stays quiet.

## Where to go next

If you've just arrived, [install it and sign in](/help/install-and-sign-in), then
[write your first entry](/help/your-first-entry). If you're coming from another
journal, [bring it with you](/help/bring-your-journal) — nothing has to start
from an empty page.
