Three of the slash commands put something structured on the page. They're what
lets Dayspring read your journal back to you later.

## `/scripture`

Type `/scripture` (or just `/scr`) and search for a passage. Pick one and the
text lands on the page — the actual words, not a reference you have to look up
later.

Passages come from the ESV.

You don't have to use the command. If you simply type "Psalm 34:18" in a
sentence, Dayspring notices the reference when the entry saves and underlines
it quietly. Either way it reaches [the Lamp](/help/the-lamp).

## `/pray`

Logs a prayer. Write it as you'd pray it — plainly.

What this gives you is [the Altar](/help/the-altar): months later, the things
you've prayed about are gathered by subject, with whatever you wrote afterwards
sitting alongside. Not "prayers answered: 4". Just the prayer, and the rest of
the story you wrote without realising it was the rest of the story.

## `/sense`

For a word or an impression — the thing you'd otherwise not write down because
it felt too slight, or because writing it felt presumptuous.

It's kept separate from your prose deliberately, so that returning to it later
you can see it for what it was: something you sensed on a particular day, not a
conclusion you'd reached.

## `/ritual`

Opens the practice library — {{counts.practices}} contemplative practices, from
the Daily Examen to Lectio Divina to Wesley's Questions. Pick one and its
prompts appear on the page to write into.

The prompts are scaffolding, not content: **only what you type is saved**. If
you write two lines and abandon the rest, your entry contains two lines, not a
half-finished form.

See [the practice library](/help/practices) for the full list.

## These blocks are just text

Everything here stays editable. Click into a scripture block and change it,
delete a prayer, rewrite a sense. Nothing is locked, and it all exports as plain
Markdown with the rest of your writing.
