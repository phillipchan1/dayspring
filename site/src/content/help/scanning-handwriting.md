If you keep a paper journal as well, you can bring a page across without typing
it out.

Tap the **scan** button in the bar above the keyboard, photograph the page, and
Dayspring reads the handwriting into text.

## How it goes

1. Tap scan. The camera opens.
2. Photograph the page. Add more shots for a long entry — up to
   **{{limits.scan-max-pages}} pages** in one go.
3. Wait a moment while it's read.
4. **Check the draft.** It's shown to you as editable text before anything is
   saved.
5. Insert it where your cursor is.

## It transcribes, it doesn't tidy

The draft is what you wrote, as you wrote it — including the bits that don't
scan well and the sentence you abandoned halfway. Nothing is smoothed out,
improved, or rephrased.

That's the point. A tidied-up version of your journal isn't your journal.

Which is also why step 4 exists: handwriting is hard, and you should see what it
made of yours before it becomes part of your record.

## Getting a better read

- Flat page, no curl at the spine.
- Even light. Avoid your own shadow across the page.
- Get the whole page in frame rather than zooming in close.

## Where the button is

Same place as [the microphone](/help/dictating-an-entry) — the bar above the
on-screen keyboard. So this is an iPhone and iPad feature; there's no equivalent
in the Mac app.

## Afterwards

Once inserted, the text is an ordinary entry. Any Bible references in it get
noticed and reach [the Lamp](/help/the-lamp) exactly as if you'd typed them —
which means a paper journal you photograph over a few evenings joins the rest of
your history properly.

Give it the right date via **Change date** in the entry menu, so it lands where
it belongs rather than today.
