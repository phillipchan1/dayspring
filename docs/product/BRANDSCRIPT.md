# BrandScript — StoryBrand SB7

Donald Miller's framework, applied to Dayspring. The core discipline: **the customer
is the hero, we are the guide.** Every time our copy makes Dayspring the hero
("powerful AI-driven insights"), we've broken the story.

Draft 1, 2026-07-26. The customer's own words from the `PERSONAS.md` interview script
should replace ours wherever they're better — and they usually will be.

---

## 1. The Character

**A practicing Christian who wants their walk with God to be one continuous story
rather than a pile of forgotten moments.**

What they want, stated the way they'd state it: *"I want to know that I'm actually
going somewhere with God — and I want to remember what He's already done."*

Two entry points to the same character (see `PERSONAS.md`):
- **The Archivist** — years of journal history they've never once re-read.
- **The Dry Season** — can't tell if anything is happening at all.

---

## 2. The Problem

**The villain: forgetting.**

Not busyness, not distraction, not the phone. *Forgetting.* It's the right villain
because it's the true one, and because Scripture treats it as the recurring failure of
God's people — Israel doesn't stop believing God exists, they stop remembering what He
did. Every altar and pile of stones in the Old Testament is a countermeasure against
this exact enemy.

Naming forgetting as the villain also explains the product's own vocabulary: the
Covenant sky, cairns, remembrance. The features *are* the counterattack.

**External problem** *(what's tangibly wrong)*
My journal entries disappear into a pile I never read again. Years of writing, and I
couldn't tell you what's in it.

**Internal problem** *(how that makes them feel — the real driver of purchase)*
I can't tell if I'm growing. God feels distant, and I have no way of knowing whether
that's a season or a slide. I've forgotten most of what He's done for me, which means
I'm facing today's fear without yesterday's evidence.

**Philosophical problem** *(why this is just plain wrong)*
**Remembering what God has done is a spiritual discipline, not nostalgia.** A life of
faith is meant to accumulate — testimony on testimony, answer on answer. It's wrong
that the most meaningful writing a person does is the writing they're most likely to
lose. Faithfulness deserves to be remembered.

> Most journaling apps have no philosophical problem at all — they're storage. This
> is the strongest asset in the BrandScript. It should show up in the copy far more
> than it currently does.

---

## 3. The Guide

**Empathy:**
*"You've written for years and never gone back. Not because you didn't care — because
there was no way in. A pile of entries isn't a story, and scrolling isn't remembering."*

**Authority** *(what earns the right — honest about our stage)*:
- Built by one person who journals daily and imported 3,460 of his own entries into it
  before asking anyone else to.
- **Grounded by architecture, not by promise:** facts are computed in code, quotes are
  verbatim from your entries, and the model only ever selects. It cannot invent a
  memory. *(This is the single most persuasive authority claim we have — it's a
  structural guarantee, not a marketing adjective.)*
- Deliberate refusals as credentials: no streaks, no scores, no sermonizing, no verdict
  on your spiritual life. What we *won't* do is the proof we understand the stakes.
- A real editor — the kind people who care about writing tools recognize on sight.

**Authority we do not yet have and must not imply:** user count, testimonials,
theological endorsement, institutional backing. Get testimonials from the five
interviews; until then, don't fake social proof.

---

## 4. The Plan

**Process plan — three steps, no more:**

1. **Bring in your history.** Day One, Diarly, or start fresh today.
2. **Write like you already do.** Nothing to learn, no system to adopt.
3. **See what's been happening.** Weekly, monthly, and yearly — in your own words.

**Agreement plan — the promises that dissolve fear:**

- Your entries are yours. We never train on them, and we never sell them.
- Nothing is invented. Every reflection traces to something you actually wrote.
- Your original dates are preserved exactly. Re-importing never duplicates.
- You can export everything, any time, in plain markdown. No hostages.
- We will never score your spiritual life.

That last one is a differentiator hiding as a policy. Say it on the marketing site.

**Direct:** *Begin your 14-day free trial* — currently live in `PaywallScreen.tsx`. Keep it.

**Transitional** *(for the not-yet-ready — currently the biggest gap in the funnel)*:

  **"Read your Day One archive."** Free one-page reflection generated from an export,
  no account. It demonstrates grounding, targets P1 precisely, and tests Bet 3 from

## 6. Success — what life looks like if they buy

They open Dayspring in January of their third year and see the whole thing: the
matters they carried and which ones God resolved, the seasons they climbed, the verses
that kept finding them, the prayers they'd forgotten and the answers they never
connected.

Nothing was invented. All of it was theirs. None of it was reachable before.

They cry a little. They send it to their spouse.

**Identity transformation:**

| From | To |
|---|---|
| Someone who writes and forgets | Someone who remembers |
| Unsure whether they're growing | Able to see the shape of it |
| Carrying today's fear alone | Carrying it with yesterday's evidence |
| Years of entries as dead storage | Years of entries as testimony |

---

## 7. Failure — what's at stake if they don't

Another five years of faithful writing that no one, including them, will ever read.
The answers arrive and go unnoticed because the asking was forgotten. A record of
God's faithfulness that functions as an archive nobody opens.

**Tone caution:** with this audience, stakes must be stated *once*, gently, and never
weaponized. Fear-based copy about your relationship with God is a Principle 2
violation — that's sermonizing with a conversion goal attached. State the stakes and
move on.

## The one-liner

> **You've been writing for years. Dayspring is the first journal that reads it back
> to you — so you can finally see what God has been making of you.**

Alternates to test in interview Q11:
- *"A journal that remembers, so you don't have to."*
- *"Your faithfulness, finally legible."*
- *"Ten years of journaling. One story you've never read."*

The first is the safest and most concrete. Prefer it until real users hand us better.

## The grunt test

A caveman lands on the page. In five seconds, can he grunt:

1. **What do you offer?** A journal that reads your history back to you.
2. **How will it make my life better?** I'll see how God has been working — and stop
   forgetting what He's done.
3. **What do I do to buy it?** Start a free trial. *(Button, above the fold, repeated.)*

**Current site status: unassessed.** Run this test against the live marketing page and
record the result here — a failed grunt test at #2 is the most common and most costly
failure mode, and "what God has been making of you" is beautiful but may not survive
five seconds cold.

## Language rules

**Words we use:** remember, faithfulness, testimony, season, carry, notice, evidence,
history, your own words, over time, God has been.

**Words we never use:** *journey* (exhausted), *unlock* / *unleash* / *supercharge*
(SaaS register), *AI-powered* (it's an implementation detail and it frightens this
audience), *insights* as a noun-blob, *optimize* / *track* / *streak* / *score*,
*mindfulness* / *wellness* (wrong tradition), *hack*.

**Voice:** warm, sacred, plain-spoken. Never sermonize, never gamify.
*(Verbatim from `onboardingCopy.ts:2` — it was right the first time.)*

**The name.** Dayspring — Luke 1:78, *"the dayspring from on high hath visited us."*
Already in `PaywallScreen.tsx`. It carries first light, visitation, and mercy after
darkness, which is precisely the promise. Use it more; most users won't know the
reference and telling them is a small gift.
