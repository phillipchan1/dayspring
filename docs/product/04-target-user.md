## Primary user
**"The reflective practitioner."** A committed Christian, likely 28–55, who *already* keeps some form of spiritual journal (a physical notebook, Notes, Day One, or an examen practice). They read seriously — the kind of person who has heard of Nouwen, Peterson, or Comer. They are not looking for a habit-streak gamified devotional; they find those slightly embarrassing. **[ASSUMPTION — persona to validate via interviews; the code strongly implies this reader: Rituals library, contemplative forms, ESV, restraint in the UI. DERIVED support.]**

Not a demographic bet on "all Christians." A **psychographic** bet on *depth + habit already present*.

## Core job-to-be-done
> **"Help me write honestly about my inner life before God — and, over time, show me whether I'm actually changing."**

Two clauses, both load-bearing:
- **The daily job:** *"Give me a place to write that feels sacred and undistracting, where prayer/scripture/discernment belong on the page."* Served by the editor + spiritual blocks. **[DERIVED]**
- **The horizon job:** *"Tell me who I'm becoming — am I growing in patience, is my prayer life deepening — in a way a single day can't show me."* Served by Ascent/Lamp/Altar + compounding rollups. **[DERIVED]**

The second is the differentiated job and the retention engine. The first is what earns the right to deliver it (they must stay long enough to reach the payoff).

## The emotional wedge
Not productivity. Not "mental fitness." The feelings Dayspring trades on:
- **Being seen without being exposed** — *"some things are meant to be written before they're ever spoken aloud."* The privacy story is emotional, not technical. **[DERIVED — paywall/privacy copy.]**
- **Continuity of self** — the quiet reassurance that a scattered year actually had an arc, that you *are* being formed even when it's invisible day to day. *"A day is too small to hold a pattern; a year is a mirror."*
- **Reverence over optimization** — the app respects the writing as sacred (stewardship framing), which is the opposite of a dopamine-loop wellness app.

This emotional wedge is the hardest thing for a secular incumbent to copy authentically — which is why it, not the feature list, is the core of the target-user bet.

## Segments explicitly deferred

- **Secular reflective journalers** — reachable later by softening faith language, but chasing them now dilutes the signal that wins the core. **Defer.**
- **Casual/new Christians wanting devotionals & streaks** — served better (and more cheaply) by Hallow/YouVersion; wrong tone for this product. **Defer, likely permanently.**
