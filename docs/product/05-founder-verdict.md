## Top 3 risks
1. **Retention / the payoff-timing trap (highest).** The hero payoff (year-in-review) arrives *after* the point where most journaling apps have already lost the user. Journaling is a notoriously high-churn habit. If people don't survive to the mirror, the whole thesis doesn't fire. *Import mitigates this for switchers* (they arrive with history) **[DERIVED]** — but for net-new journalers it's the existential risk. **Mitigation: make month-one indispensable on the writing experience alone; lean on import to deliver an instant look-back.**
2. **Feature-vs-product.** Dayspring is a bundle of excellent features. The risk is that it's a beautiful *feature set* rather than a *product with one indispensable job* — and that a bigger player (YouVersion, Day One) ships the one feature that matters (faith year-in-review) at massive reach. **Mitigation: own the emotional/writing relationship and the brand, which reach can't copy.**
3. **Defensibility / it's copyable.** As doc 03 concedes, the features are clonable in a quarter. The moat is voice + taste + trust, which are real but fragile — one privacy misstep or one tonal wrong-turn toward "cheesy Christian app" erases it. **Mitigation: obsessive brand consistency and a spotless, honestly-stated privacy posture (already the stance in the 💸 doc).**

## What would have to be true for this to deserve serious time
Treat these as go/no-go gates, in order:
1. **The target pays.** Evidence that practicing, journaling Christians will actually buy software (not default to free). **[NEEDS-RESEARCH → cheapest test: a small paid-acquisition + landing-page conversion probe.]**
2. **They stay.** A cohort holds meaningfully better D90/12-month retention than category baseline — ideally with the year-in-review visibly lifting it. **[NEEDS-RESEARCH → instrument cohorts now.]**
3. **They can be reached cheaply.** At least one channel (faith podcast/newsletter/creator) shows CAC comfortably below ~$60 LTV. **[NEEDS-RESEARCH.]**
4. **The SAM clears low-thousands of reachable payers** at minimum for Path A, or tens of thousands for Path B. **[NEEDS-RESEARCH.]**

If 1–3 come back positive, this is clearly worth serious time as a Path A business with Path B positioning. If retention (2) fails, no amount of positioning saves it — fix the product before the marketing.
