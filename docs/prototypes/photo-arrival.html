Editor · photos

Five photos, set down on a page

The app starts work the instant you let go. For most of that work it shows you an empty grey box and no words. The photo is already on your device the whole time, and we don't draw it.

What happens today

Read from attachmentDropExtension.ts, attachmentQueue.ts, attachments.ts and attachmentImageExtension.ts. Photos are handled one at a time, so steps 3 to 6 repeat for each.

  1. What the app doesWhat you see
  2. 1. Puts five placeholder lines on the page, at once.Five empty boxes, all the same 4:3 shape, pulsing faintly. No words. The word "uploading…" is only drawn for a photo on its own, never inside a set.
  3. 2. Asks the server who is signed in. Once for the batch, then again for every photo.The same boxes.
  4. 3. Reads the photo's date and place, shrinks it to 2400px, re-encodes it, opens it a second time for its colour, fingerprints it. All on the same thread you type on.The same boxes. Typing can catch.
  5. 4. Checks whether it is already stored, sends it, writes its record. The other four wait in line.The same boxes. Nothing says which one, or how many are left.
  6. 5. Swaps the placeholder for the real photo, then asks storage for that photo back.Its box stops pulsing and goes flat. It looks finished and empty.
  7. 6. The picture arrives and its real shape is learned.The photo fades in and the rows jump as the 4:3 box becomes the real shape.

Three ways to show it

All three share one change underneath: the photo is drawn from your own device the moment you drop it, in its real shape, so nothing jumps later. They differ only in how saving is shown.

What I would build

A, with its one line of words. The photo is on the page the instant you let go, and it comes into colour as it is saved, so each photo shows its own progress and no control is added to the writing surface. The line under the set gives the count for anyone who wants it, then turns back into the set's ordinary caption.

B is the quietest and a fair second. C shows the most detail and puts the most marks on the page, which is the opposite of how the editor behaves everywhere else.

What changes underneath, whichever you pick

  • Draw the dropped file straight away from the device, in its real shape. This removes the empty boxes, the flat gap in step 5, and the jump in step 6.
  • Keep showing that local picture after the save finishes, so the app never asks storage for a photo it is already holding.
  • Ask who is signed in once per batch, from the session already in memory.
  • Save two or three photos side by side. Five photos then take about as long as two.
  • Report real progress from the send, so A's colour and B's line move with the bytes.
  • Offline keeps working as it does now. The line says the photos are waiting and are safe on this device.
