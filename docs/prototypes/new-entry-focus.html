Arriving to write

In Diarly, ⌘N opens a new entry and goes straight to focus: everything else disappears until you move to the left edge. Below are four ways Dayspring could handle the same moment. Pick one and try it in the window.

    Your browser keeps ⌘N for itself, so use the button. Inside the window, Esc and ⌘⏎ behave as they do in the app.

    Why Settle over Diarly's version

    • The feeling you liked comes from having nothing else on screen once the words start. Settle gives you that without a mode, so there is no state to get lost in.
    • Diarly's ⌘N would hide the ritual shelf on the one page where it lives. Rituals are a front-door feature now (D-001), and this would remove them from every ⌘N.
    • Ascent, Lamp and Altar are what you pay for. A rail that disappears after every new entry is a rail people stop seeing.
    • Fading keeps every element in place, so the line you are typing never shifts. Unmounting the rail re-centers the column under your caret.
    • iA Writer and Ulysses do the same thing: chrome recedes while you type and comes back when you reach for it.

    And for the Diarly people

    If someone wants the full Diarly behavior, give them a setting, with the ritual shelf kept and the edge peek added. That is the "Focus, with doors" variant.

    When you start a new entry

    The rail and top bar fade as you type and come back when you move the pointer.

    Verdict: test first, on alpha

    Do now, on stable: record when focus mode is entered and left, with the route in (⌘⏎, button, or new entry) and the seconds since the last ⌘N. If people already press ⌘⏎ right after ⌘N, that is the demand, measured without changing anything.

    Do on alpha: ship Settle as the default and "Go straight to focus" as a setting. Write in it for two weeks.

    Kill Settle if: the fade flickers or distracts in real writing (any pointer nudge brings chrome back), or visits to Ascent, Lamp and Altar per writing day drop once it is on. Keep "straight to focus" off by default if: fewer than about one in ten desktop writers ever use ⌘⏎ today.
