The library should know what hour it is

This isn't new art. Every Dayspring theme already defines --journal-glow — a light source parked at 50% -30%, just above the top edge of the page. The app has had a sky the whole time. It has just never moved. All the hour does is give that light a position and a warmth, so the theme is tinted rather than replaced. Scrub the clock, then switch themes: a nocturne morning is first amber at a black horizon, and it is still nocturne.

The sky is composed, never swapped

Two layers sit behind the library. The lower one is the theme’s own --journal-glow, untouched — that’s why a cloister morning still reads as cloister. The upper one is the only thing the clock controls: a single radial gradient whose position and hue come from the band, laid over the theme’s light rather than in place of it.

That distinction matters more than it looks. Someone who chose nocturne chose pure black on purpose. Replacing it with a bright morning at 7am overrules them in their own journal.

Four bands, named for the hours

Dawn (4–10): light low and to the left, warm, rising. Midday (11–16): high and centred — exactly where the app already puts it — pale and small. Evening (17–21): low and right, deeper. Night (22–3): the overhead light nearly gone, a cool wash from the lower edge, and on dark themes a still star field. Nothing twinkles; that would be the app performing.

The greeting states a fact about the world

“Good morning” is a fact about the clock. “You usually write in the mornings” would be a claim about the writer — and a claim about the writer’s devotional habits is the streak this product has already refused (Principle 2).

The line under the title is the existing .practice-library__because slot, whose comment in the source already reads “never a claim about the writer — only about where they opened this from.” The clock is the same kind of fact. And at 1am it says if you want them, not you haven’t written today.

Mid-entry still wins the filter

Opening the library from inside an entry already defaults to Need-based, because something surfaced while writing. That signal is stronger than the clock and keeps priority.

But the sky and the greeting still follow the hour — the sky is about the world, the filter is about you. Every filter stays one tap away; this reorders and hides nothing.
