Privacy

the dayspring from on high

Last updated 26 July 2026

Dayspring is a private journal. People write things here they would not write anywhere else. This page describes exactly what happens to those words — what is stored, what leaves your device, and who else ever sees it.

What we store

  • Your account. An email address and, if your sign-in provider supplies one, a display name. Nothing else. We never see your password — sign-in happens through Google or Apple.
  • What you write. Entries, prayers, scripture references, reminders, and anything the app derives from them. This is stored in our database so it is there on every device you sign in from.
  • Images and audio you add. Photos attached to entries, pages you scan, and voice recordings you dictate.
  • Settings and preferences. Theme, fonts, and similar. Not sensitive, but it is yours.

Everything is stored with Supabase (hosted on Amazon Web Services) and is scoped to your account at the database level — one person's rows are not readable by another's session, enforced by the database rather than by application code.

What leaves your device, and where it goes

Dayspring uses outside services for a few specific jobs. Each one receives only what that job needs.

  • OpenAI — the features that read what you wrote. Reflections, insights, search, transcribing your dictation, and reading a scanned handwritten page all send the relevant text, audio, or image to OpenAI's API to be processed, then send the result back. This is the one place your actual words are handled by a third party. OpenAI's API terms state that data sent through the API is not used to train their models.
  • Crossway (the ESV API) — looks up Bible passages. It receives only the reference itself, like "John 3:16". It never receives anything you wrote.
  • Stripe — handles subscriptions on the web and desktop. Card details go directly to Stripe and never touch our servers; we store only a customer id.
  • Apple — handles subscriptions purchased inside the iOS app. Same arrangement: Apple processes the payment, we are told only whether you are subscribed.

That is the complete list. Your journal is not sent anywhere else, and it is never sold, rented, or shared for advertising. We do not run ads.

Usage statistics

Dayspring can record anonymous counts of which features get used — that a scripture search happened, not what was searched for. The design makes this structural rather than a promise: the event system has no free-text field anywhere in it, so the content of an entry physically cannot travel through it.

You can turn this off entirely in Settings → About → Privacy. When it is off, nothing is recorded and nothing is sent.

On your device

The app keeps a local copy of your journal so it opens instantly and keeps working offline. Signing out erases that local copy.

Deleting your account

Settings → About → Delete account. This permanently erases your entries, prayers, scripture references, images, and account record, and cancels any subscription billed through us. It is immediate and cannot be undone — we cannot recover a deleted journal, including at your own later request.

If you subscribed through the App Store, Apple handles that subscription and only you can cancel it, from Settings → Apple Account → Subscriptions on your device. Deleting your Dayspring account does not cancel it.

You can export your journal at any time before deleting it, from Settings → Import & backup.

Children

Dayspring is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect their information.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that affects how your writing is handled, we will say so in the app rather than quietly editing this page.
